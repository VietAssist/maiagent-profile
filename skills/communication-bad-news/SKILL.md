# SKILL: Delivering bad news or delays

## When to use this skill
When the user needs to tell a client or manager about a missed deadline, a problem, a mistake, or unexpected bad news.

## Core principle
Bad news delivered well preserves trust. The goal is to be honest, take responsibility where appropriate, and immediately focus on the solution — not the problem.

## Structure for bad news emails
1. **State the situation clearly** — don't bury the lead or make them read to the end to find out what happened
2. **Take responsibility** where appropriate — avoid blame-shifting
3. **Explain briefly** — one or two sentences on what happened, not a long excuse
4. **Pivot immediately to the solution or next step** — this is the most important part
5. **Confirm what you will do and by when** — be specific

## Tone calibration
- Own the issue without over-dramatising: "I want to let you know that..." not "I deeply regret to inform you..."
- Avoid defensive language: don't start with "Due to unforeseen circumstances..."
- Sound calm and in control — panic in an email makes clients panic

## Common Vietnamese writer mistakes to fix
- Writing a long apology before getting to the point
- Being vague about what actually went wrong
- Not offering a concrete next step or timeline

## Output format
Write the complete email. Flag to the user if the situation requires a phone or video call instead of just email.
