# SKILL: Follow-up email

## When to use this skill
When the user needs to follow up on an unanswered email, a pending decision, a proposal, or an overdue response — without sounding pushy or needy.

## Core principle
A good follow-up is short, assumes good faith, and makes it easy for the reader to respond. Never guilt-trip, never apologise for following up.

## Follow-up structure
1. **One-line opener**: reference the previous message naturally ("Just circling back on my email from [day]")
2. **Restate the ask in one sentence**: don't make them scroll back
3. **Give an easy out or deadline if relevant**: "Happy to jump on a quick call if easier"
4. **Short close**: "Thanks" or "Looking forward to your thoughts"

## Tone rules
- Never: "I just wanted to check in..." (weak) → use "I'm following up on..."
- Never: "Sorry to bother you again" → remove all apology language
- Never guilt: "I haven't heard back from you..." → assume they're busy, not ignoring
- Keep it shorter than the original email

## Timing guidance (share with user if they ask)
- First follow-up: 3–5 business days after no response
- Second follow-up: 5–7 days after first follow-up
- Third follow-up: final — make it clear this is the last nudge

## Output format
Write the complete follow-up email, ready to send. Keep it under 5 sentences where possible.
