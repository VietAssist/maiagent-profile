# SKILL: Handling a difficult client conversation

## When to use this skill
When the user is dealing with a client who is angry, unreasonable, making unfair demands, or where the relationship is under stress.

## Core principle
The goal is not to win the argument — it is to resolve the situation while preserving your professionalism and, where possible, the relationship.

## Types of difficult situations and how to handle each

### Angry or frustrated client
1. Acknowledge their frustration first — do not defend immediately
2. Separate emotion from the facts
3. State clearly what you will do to resolve it
4. Do not match their emotional energy in writing

Script: "I understand you're frustrated with [situation] and I take that seriously. Here's what I'm going to do: [specific action] by [specific date]. Please let me know if that works for you."

### Client making unreasonable demands (outside scope)
1. Acknowledge the request
2. Clearly reference the agreed scope
3. Offer to do the additional work at an additional cost
4. Stay calm — this is a business boundary, not a personal conflict

Script: "I'd be happy to help with [request]. That falls outside our original scope, so I'd handle it as an additional piece of work at [rate/cost]. Would you like me to send a quick quote?"

### Client who keeps changing the brief
1. Document every change in writing immediately
2. Flag the impact on timeline or cost
3. Ask them to confirm the final direction before you proceed

Script: "Just to confirm — the new direction is [X]. This will add [X days / $X] to the project. Please confirm and I'll get started."

### Client who is not responding
See follow-up email skill.

## Rules for difficult client communication
- Always communicate in writing so there is a record
- Stay calm and factual — no sarcasm, no frustration in the text
- Never insult or blame the client directly, even if they are wrong
- Know when to walk away — some clients are not worth keeping

## Output format
Help the user draft the specific message for their situation. Ask: what happened, what does the user want the outcome to be?
