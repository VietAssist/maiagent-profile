# SKILL: Understanding Western work culture norms

## When to use this skill
When the user is confused by a Western colleague's behaviour, unsure how to handle a cultural situation, or wants to understand unwritten rules of Western professional culture.

## Key cultural differences to explain when relevant

### Directness
Western professionals (especially American, Australian, Northern European) are direct. "I don't think this will work" is not rude — it is efficient. Vietnamese culture uses more indirect communication. When a Western colleague gives you direct feedback, it is usually not personal.

### Disagreeing with authority
In Western workplaces it is normal and expected to disagree with a manager — respectfully. Staying silent when you disagree is often seen as having no opinion or not being engaged.

### Asking questions
Asking clarifying questions signals intelligence and engagement in Western culture. It does not signal incompetence. Staying silent to save face and then making mistakes is far worse.

### Time and deadlines
"I'll get it to you by Friday" means Friday. If this changes, communicate early — before the deadline, not after. Western clients see proactive communication as professionalism.

### Small talk
Western calls often start with 2–3 minutes of small talk (weather, weekend, sports). This is not a waste of time — it is relationship building. Jumping straight to business can seem cold.

### Email tone
Direct email ≠ rude email in Western culture. A short, clear email is a sign of respect for the reader's time.

### Feedback culture
Western teams give and receive direct feedback regularly. "Your report needs more data" is not an attack on you as a person.

## How to handle cultural confusion
When the user describes a situation they don't understand, explain:
1. What the Western cultural norm is in that situation
2. How it differs from Vietnamese norms
3. How the user should respond or what they should expect

## Output format
Explain the cultural context clearly and practically. Give the user a concrete way to respond or behave in that situation.
