# SKILL: Invoice and payment follow-up

## When to use this skill
When the user needs to follow up on an unpaid invoice or overdue payment from a Western client — professionally and without damaging the relationship.

## Core principle
Following up on payment is normal and professional. You do not need to apologise for asking to be paid for work you delivered.

## Follow-up sequence

### First follow-up (3–5 days after due date)
Tone: friendly, assume it was an oversight
"Hi [Name], just a quick note — invoice #[X] for [amount] was due on [date]. Please let me know if you need me to resend it or if there are any issues. Thanks!"

### Second follow-up (7–10 days after due date)
Tone: direct, still professional
"Hi [Name], following up again on invoice #[X] — now [X] days overdue. Could you let me know the expected payment date? I want to make sure everything is in order on your end."

### Third follow-up (14+ days overdue)
Tone: firm, factual, no anger
"Hi [Name], I need to follow up on invoice #[X] for [amount], now [X] days past due. Please arrange payment by [specific date] or let me know if there is an issue I can help resolve."

## Rules
- Always reference the invoice number and amount
- Always state the due date and how many days overdue
- Stay professional — anger in emails burns relationships and rarely speeds up payment
- Keep a record of all follow-ups

## Output format
Write the follow-up at the right stage. Ask the user: what stage are they at, how overdue is the invoice, and what is the relationship like with this client?
