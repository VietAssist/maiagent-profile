# SKILL: Asking for testimonials or referrals

## When to use this skill
When the user wants to ask a satisfied client for a written testimonial, a LinkedIn recommendation, or a referral to other potential clients.

## Core principle
The best time to ask is when the client is happiest — at project completion or right after a win. A warm, specific ask gets a much better result than a generic one.

## Asking for a testimonial

### Message structure
1. Reference a specific positive moment or result from the project
2. Mention that you are building your portfolio/profile
3. Ask specifically — give them a format so it is easy for them
4. Optional: offer to draft something for them to edit (makes it much more likely to happen)

### Example
"Hi [Name], it was great working with you on [project] — especially seeing [specific result]. I'm updating my portfolio and would love to include a short testimonial from you if you're open to it. Even 2–3 sentences about your experience would be really helpful. Happy to draft something for you to tweak if that's easier!"

## Asking for a referral

### Message structure
1. Reference the successful work you did together
2. Mention you are taking on new clients
3. Ask if they know anyone who might benefit from your work
4. Make it easy — give them a one-line description of who you help

### Example
"Hi [Name], I really enjoyed our work together on [project]. I'm currently taking on new clients and wondered if you know anyone who might benefit from [what you do]. Even a quick introduction would mean a lot!"

## Rules
- Never ask for both a testimonial and a referral in the same message — choose one
- Make it as easy as possible for them — draft it, give a format, reduce friction
- Timing matters — ask when the relationship is warm, not months later

## Output format
Write the complete message. Ask the user: which are they asking for, and what was the project or result they can reference?
