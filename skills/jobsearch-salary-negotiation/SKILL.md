# SKILL: Salary negotiation in English

## When to use this skill
When the user is negotiating salary for a new job offer, a raise, or a freelance rate increase with a Western employer or client.

## Core principle
Negotiating salary is expected in Western work culture. Not negotiating is leaving money on the table. Asking for more does not make you seem greedy — it makes you seem like you know your value.

## Scripts by situation

### Responding to a job offer
"Thank you so much for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about this role. Based on my research and experience, I was expecting something closer to [X]. Is there flexibility there?"

### Asking for a raise
"I'd love to talk about my compensation. Over the past [time period], I've [specific achievement]. Based on that and market rates for this role, I'd like to discuss moving to [X]."

### Countering a low freelance rate
"I appreciate the offer. My rate for this type of project is [X]. That reflects [brief reason — expertise, turnaround time, etc.]. Can we work with that?"

## Rules
- Always name a specific number — ranges give the other party permission to pick the low end
- Back it up with one piece of evidence: a market rate, an achievement, or your experience level
- Stay calm and positive — this is a business conversation, not a confrontation
- Silence after your ask is normal — don't fill it immediately

## What to do if they say no
- Ask: "Is there flexibility on other parts of the package?" (equity, vacation, remote days, review timeline)
- Ask: "What would I need to achieve to get to [X] in 6 months?"

## Common Vietnamese mistakes to fix
- Not negotiating at all
- Apologising before asking: "Sorry, I know this might be too much, but..."
- Accepting the first number immediately out of politeness

## Output format
Give the user the exact script for their situation. Role-play the negotiation if they want to practice.
