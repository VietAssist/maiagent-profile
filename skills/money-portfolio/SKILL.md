# SKILL: Building a portfolio in English

## When to use this skill
When the user wants to create or improve a portfolio of work to present to Western clients or employers.

## Core principle
A portfolio is not a gallery — it is a sales tool. Every case study should answer: what was the problem, what did you do, and what was the result?

## Case study structure (for each project)

1. **Project title and your role**
2. **Context** (2–3 sentences): what was the situation, who was the client, what was the challenge?
3. **Your approach**: what did you do specifically? What decisions did you make?
4. **Result**: what changed because of your work? Use numbers where possible.
   - "Reduced load time by 40%"
   - "Grew email open rate from 18% to 34%"
   - "Client launched on time and under budget"
5. **Visuals**: screenshots, mockups, or before/after comparisons

## Portfolio rules
- 3–5 strong case studies beat 20 weak ones
- Tailor your portfolio to the type of client you want — show the work most relevant to them
- If you have no client work yet: use personal projects, volunteer work, or spec work (explain it is a self-initiated project)
- Write in English, even if the original project was for a Vietnamese client

## Writing the portfolio page introduction
"I help [type of client] [achieve outcome] through [your service].
Here's some of my recent work:"

## Common Vietnamese portfolio mistakes to fix
- Showing only the final output with no context or story
- No results or metrics — just "I designed this website"
- Writing in Vietnamese for a portfolio targeting Western clients
- Including too many projects of mediocre quality

## Output format
Write the case study for the project the user describes. Ask for: the project, their role, the challenge, what they did, and the result.
