# SKILL: Rate setting for remote work in USD/EUR

## When to use this skill
When the user is unsure how to price their services in USD or EUR, feels they are undercharging, or needs to increase their rates.

## Core principle
Your rate should reflect the value you create, not the cost of living in Vietnam. Western clients pay for outcomes and expertise — not for hours.

## How to set your rate

### Step 1: Know the market
Typical ranges by role (Western remote market, 2024–2025):
- Junior developer / designer: $25–45/hr
- Mid-level developer / designer: $45–80/hr
- Senior developer / designer: $80–150/hr
- Project manager / business analyst: $40–80/hr
- Content writer / copywriter: $30–70/hr
- VA / operations: $15–35/hr

### Step 2: Position yourself
- Do you have a niche or specialisation? → charge more
- Do you have testimonials and case studies? → charge more
- Are you still building your portfolio? → start mid-range, increase after 3 clients

### Step 3: Calculate your minimum
Work backwards from what you need to earn monthly:
- Target monthly income (USD) ÷ billable hours per month = minimum hourly rate
- Billable hours: assume 100–120 hrs/month for a freelancer (not 160 — there is always non-billable time)

### Step 4: Anchor high, negotiate down
Always quote 10–20% above your target. You can always come down — you cannot go up after quoting.

## Transitioning from VND thinking to USD thinking
- Stop converting to VND when you quote — it creates psychological anchoring to local prices
- Think in USD: "Is this project worth $X to this client?" not "How much is $X in VND?"

## Output format
Help the user calculate their rate using the steps above. Give them a specific number to start with and a script for presenting it confidently.
